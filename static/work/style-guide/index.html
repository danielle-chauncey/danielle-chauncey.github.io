Use Case-Led Style Guide
for a Solutions Library

The naming, writing, and diagram standards for a cloud solutions library: implementation guides, architecture diagrams, and solution pages written by 300+ solutions architects and specialists. Internal tools, names, and approval paths are removed. The rules themselves are unchanged.
The one rule

It is product information, not marketing copy. A reader who is not technical should be able to tell from one page whether a solution fits their organization. Every rule in this guide serves that test.

Four principles
01PlainUse the words a customer types into a search engine. Define a term or drop it.
02BriefPay for every word. 25 words or fewer per sentence. The point goes first.
03SpecificName the component, the number, and the source. No weasel words or unsourced superlatives.
04UsableWrite for people who skim. Lists over blocks, real headings, descriptive links.

Who it serves
Solutions architects and specialists who build architectures and write the content that explains them. Most are engineers first and writers second. That is why every rule here comes with a correct and an incorrect example: contributors learn faster by comparing two versions than by reading a principle.

01Taxonomy

Start here

The library holds four solution types: platform services, packaged solutions, partner solutions, and guidance. Each one maps to a use case. Each use case sits in a solution area, and each solution area sits in a domain. Name from the top down: the use case name should fit its solution area, and the solution area should reflect its domain.

Level 1DomainAn industry or a technology field.Financial Services
Level 2Solution areaRelated use cases for one buyer or one technology category.Trade Lifecycle Modernization
Level 3Use caseOne customer problem or opportunity.Regulatory Reporting
Level 4Solution typeThe asset: solution, guidance, partner offer, or service.Guidance for Regulatory Reporting on Northwind

Industry domains can add a sub-domain for a customer segment, such as Financial Services › Capital Markets.

02Naming formats

Titles are search terms

Customers were confused about what counts as a "solution," because many assets used the word. One naming system fixed that. Use these formats for every new title.

Content typeFormatLimitExamples
Solution areaNouns for a set of related use cases. Industry areas align to a business function. Technology areas align to a technical category.~50 charsPrivacy-Enhanced Data Collaboration
Language Understanding
Use caseNouns for the customer problem or opportunity~50 charsCustomer 360 Profiles
Data Clean Rooms
Solution[Use Case] on Northwind or [Use Case] with/for [Service]. With a third party: [Use Case] for [Third Party] on Northwind~55 charsAutomated Data Analytics on Northwind
Data Analytics for Acme on Northwind
GuidanceGuidance for [Use Case] on Northwind or using/with [Service]. Extra room for the required words.~70 charsGuidance for Contextual Intelligence for Advertising on Northwind
PartnerThe partner names its own offer. Suggested patterns: [Product], [Product] [Use Case], [Use Case] with [Product], [Product] for [Service]Partner’s callEdit in the Cloud with Acme
Every title
  • Plain, descriptive nouns. No hype or claims you cannot prove.
  • Title case. "And," never an ampersand.
  • No slashes or dashes.
  • One form of an acronym. Introduce the other in the first sentence of the page.
  • Check search volume before you pick between two terms.
Why limits matter
Titles render on library cards, search results, and navigation. Past about 50 characters they wrap or truncate, and the words that get cut are usually the ones a customer searched for. Limits exclude spaces unless the rule says otherwise.

04Naming solutions, guidance, and partner offers

Rules with examples
Packaged solutions
01A specific use case or outcomeEasy to find, and named for what deploying it gets you.
Automated Security Response on Northwind
Security on NorthwindA topic, not a problem.
02Don't sound like a serviceAvoid words that imply a product: engine, automator, core, maker, registry, detector.
Media Insights on Northwind
Northwind Media Insights Engine
03No platform brand up frontA brand prefix makes a solution read like an official service. It needs executive sign-off.
Security Automations for Northwind Firewall
Northwind Security Automations
04Keep the product's own nameA platform-owned solution that runs a third-party product uses that product's name.
Acme Server for Northwind
Running the Server for Acme on the Northwind Cloud
05One solution, one nameMultiple deployment options get descriptions, not names.
Live Streaming on NorthwindOne name, two reference architectures.
Live Streaming on Northwind and Live Streaming on Northwind with Object Storage
06One form of an acronymNo parentheses in titles.
IoT Device Simulator
Protecting Workloads from Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) Attacks
Guidance
01The full formatStarts with "Guidance for," ends with the platform or service.
Guidance for Baseline Security Assessment on Northwind
Baseline Security Assessment
02The right format is not enoughIt still needs a specific use case.
Guidance for Near Real-Time Fraud Detection on Northwind
Guidance for Security on Northwind
03Concise for searchDescriptive words, nothing extra. One acronym form.
Guidance for Building an Agricultural Sensor Network using IoT
Guidance for Securing Workloads with Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) on Northwind
Partner offers
Partners own and name their offers. Pick a name a buyer could identify on sight: the brand or manufacturer, in title case, with no hype. Putting the platform's brand on a partner offer ("on Northwind," "powered by Northwind") is reserved for strategic partners and needs executive approval.

05Three description formulas

Use case · Solution area · Guidance

Descriptions are objective and informative, never persuasive. Write for a CIO and a developer at once, at a 100 to 200 technical level. Use the SEO terms from your marketing lead.

A · Use case description · 75–80 words max
1–2 sentences · ChallengeThe problem or opportunity, without talking down to the reader.
1 sentence · Approach"[Use case] solutions on Northwind help customers..."
1–2 sentences · BenefitThe business outcome. Stay out of the technical weeds.
Banks traditionally use legacy systems for core banking applications, such as lending, payments, and deposits. However, these systems are monolithic, expensive, and lack open architecture, which slows the bank's response to market demands. Using Core Banking System Modernization solutions on Northwind, banks can replace these costly systems with core applications based on open APIs. With these solutions, banks can reduce system costs, improve their agility, and find new ways to serve their customers.
Financial Services › Banking › Core Banking System Modernization · 76 words
Leave out
  • Customer names, even if you think they would agree
  • Condescension ("Customers don't understand how to...")
  • Promises or grand statements about results
  • Calls to action ("Click to deploy...")
  • Jargon
B · Solution area description · 255 chars max
Solutions to help [persona] achieve [benefits]
Business areas name a business leader (CMO, VP of Supply Chain). Technical areas name a technical leader (CTO, VP of Engineering).
Solutions to enable the chief technology officer to create flexible, remote studios and deploy GPU-powered workstations, allowing artists and designers to work on multiple large files in the cloud.
Games › Cloud Game Development · 199 characters
C · Guidance description · customer-centric, four moves
01 · Open"This Guidance demonstrates..." plus the value to the customer, not the service.
02 · ComponentsWhat the architecture includes, in terms the target user recognizes.
03 · BenefitsSecurity, scale, cost, or operations, tied to this use case.
04 · FlexibilityHow to extend it for the customer's own needs.
This Guidance demonstrates how to deploy a resilient video discovery server architecture within a private network. The included infrastructure template provisions a pair of compute instances across two availability zones, then downloads and installs the discovery software. This foundation lets you add live video transport to your environment and supports broadcast workflows such as live cloud production. The Guidance is extensible, so you can add components or connect other services as your workflows change.

06Solution pages and architecture diagrams

Layout and visuals

Solution pages are deeply technical, so they need extra care to stay readable. Readers skim and scan. Put the important point in the first sentence of each paragraph, break text into lists, and keep each component within its limit.

Architecture diagrams
✓ Correct
  • Approved icons, all one size, every icon labeled
  • Solid black arrows, straight lines, right angles
✕ Incorrect
  • Mixed icon sizes, two unlabeled
  • Dotted, diagonal, crossing arrows
Build rules
  • Use the approved template and the current icon set only.
  • Don't crop, flip, rotate, or reshape icons.
  • Labels fit on two lines, never break mid-word, 9 pt minimum. The brand stays on the same line as the service name.
  • Short names in the diagram. Long names in the steps.
  • Flow left to right, top to bottom, or clockwise.
  • No third-party logos without written legal approval.
Checklist before you submit
  • Template and approved icons used
  • All icons the same size
  • Every icon labeled, including gateways and endpoints
  • Perpendicular arrows, none crossing
  • Every service in the diagram appears in the steps
  • Third-party approvals on file
  • File named for the title, minus "Guidance for"

07Steps, pillars, and implementation guides

The technical layer
Narrative steps
01Present tense, imperativeCommands. Never "will."
Create the database table.
You will create the database table.
02Active voiceName the actor. Cut "is used."
Capture data from social media feeds. The streaming service sends it to the cloud.
The streaming service is used to send social media data to the cloud.
03Name the reader"You" if the reader is the user. Otherwise a role, never "users."
Data scientists analyze data from the stream.
Users analyze data from the stream.

Also: define acronyms at first mention. Bold and link the full service name at first mention, then use the short name in bold.

Architecture pillars · answer what, how, and why for each of six pillars
Contributor draft · Cost optimization
WhatData integration service, key-value database
HowIntegration jobs run in the largest batch size allowed. A time-to-live (TTL) setting deletes expired items without consuming write capacity.
WhyLarge batches minimize transaction costs. TTL means you don't pay to store data you no longer need.
After the writers' edit
Integration jobs run in the largest batch size allowed to minimize transaction costs. The database's time-to-live (TTL) setting deletes expired items at an interval you set, without consuming write capacity, so you don't pay to store data you no longer need.
Three answers become one paragraph. Each fact connects to its benefit, and the "why" never stands alone.
  • Reference specific components in this architecture.
  • Give each pillar its own answer. Cost, performance, and sustainability can't all be "it scales."
  • Don't copy answers from past guidance.
  • Cut anything true of every architecture ("serverless optimizes resources").
  • Describe what exists today. No future or aspirational features.
  • Tie every answer to a business or technical benefit.
Implementation guides · flexible, but structured
StructureTitle matches the diagram. A summary answers who it helps, what it does, and why it exists. Sentence case for H2 and below. No stacked headings. Caption every table and figure.
ProceduresBreak a workflow into 7 to 10 steps, with each user decision as its own step. About 15 words per sentence, 25 max. Bold UI terms. No caps or italics for emphasis.
NestingNumbers, then lowercase letters, then roman numerals. Combine short paths with angle brackets: Settings > Roles > Create role. End with revisions and contributor names.

08Writing standards

Applies to every asset

The audience runs from developers to CIOs to curious readers, and all of them are busy. Write at an 8th-grade reading level. Research shows it helps adults read faster and retain more. That doesn't mean simpler ideas. It means the ideas cost less effort to understand.

Voice: direct, informative, approachable
FormalCasual
Friendly, but professional enough to earn trust.
PersuasiveInformative
State what it does. Let the reader decide.
Expert-onlyEveryone
A builder and a business leader both follow it.
HedgedCertain
Commit to facts. Never promise outcomes.
JargonSay what you mean or define it. "Deep dive," "out-of-the-box," and "purpose-built" are all jargon.
The dashboards let your team review the same data at once, so they can make informed decisions toward team goals.
The dashboards provide a single pane of glass to help you dive deep and move the needle.
ConcisenessImagine you pay for each word. "I emailed you," not "I wrote up an email and sent it."
Companies of all sizes want to predict future demand so they can improve the customer experience and increase revenue.
Big, medium, and small companies want to not only understand what their customers are looking for but also what they really need...
Weasel wordsVague words hide the claim.
Object storage supports variable access patterns. Storage classes set cost by access frequency: daily, monthly, or yearly.
Object storage would help with many of your challenges. You could use some features to store much of your data.
ComparativesBetter, faster, easier: than what?
This service returns results faster than comparable on-premises services.
This service gives you faster results.
SuperlativesCite a published source, or cut the claim.
According to a 2022 industry report, migration cut customer costs by 22% and raised administrator productivity by 66%.
Northwind is the cheapest cloud option with the best performance.
Active voiceSubject, verb, object. "We recommend," not "it is recommended."
The streaming service sends data from social media feeds to the cloud.
Data is captured from social media feeds and sent to the cloud.
Flag on sight

Claim carefully

ensurehelp ensure, confirm, verify

09Grammar, punctuation, accessibility

Quick reference
Point of view"You" for the reader. "We" for the company.
Sentences25 words max. One space after a period.
CapitalizationOnly proper nouns, title-case headings, and sentence starts.
DatesOctober 1, 2022 or 2022-10-01. Never 10/01/2022 or "1st."
ListsBullets for items, numbers for steps. Introduce every list with a colon.
AcronymsDefine at first mention, then abbreviate. Titles get one form only.
CommasSerial comma always. Comma before a conjunction joining two clauses, and after an intro phrase.
SemicolonsAvoid them. Write two sentences.
ColonsLowercase after one unless a full sentence follows.
SlashesOK in I/O, CI/CD, paths. Never and/or or add/edit.
RangesEn dash, no spaces: August 13–15.
SymbolsNo ampersands in body text. Straight apostrophes only.
Skip the Latin
Don't useUse instead
e.g.for example, such as
i.e.that is, specifically
etc.and so on, or cut it
ad hocone-time, when required
perfor, for each
Accessibility and inclusion
  • Descriptive link text, never "here." Device-neutral verbs: "choose," not "click."
  • Real heading levels. Bold is not a heading. Headers on every table.
  • "Preceding" and "following," not "above" and "below."
  • Simple images, even white padding, sentence-case labels.
  • Primary/replica, allowlist/denylist. No ableist or sexist terms.
