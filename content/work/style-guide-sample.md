## Content Taxonomy

All content is organized into a hierarchy: **Domain > Category > Use Case > Solution Type**. This taxonomy helps users navigate from broad business areas to specific solutions.

### Mental Model

- **Domain:** The broadest organizational level (e.g., Financial Services, Healthcare, Analytics)
- **Category:** A portfolio of related use cases aligned to a discrete customer persona or technology area
- **Use Case:** A specific customer problem or opportunity
- **Solution Type:** The actual content asset (implementation guide, architecture diagram, deployment documentation)

## Naming Guidelines

### General Principles

- Use plain, descriptive text. No persuasive marketing copy, hyperbole, or unverifiable claims.
- Use standard industry terms familiar to the customer. Consider what a customer would search for.
- Use nouns, not verbs.
- Use title case.
- Separate multiple nouns with "and" rather than an ampersand ("&").

### By Content Type

| Content Type | Format | Character Limit | Example |
|-------------|--------|-----------------|---------|
| Category | Short title describing a set of related use cases using nouns | ~50 characters | Privacy-Enhanced Data Collaboration |
| Use Case | Short title describing a customer problem or opportunity using nouns | ~50 characters | Customer 360 Profile |
| Solution | "[Use Case] on [Platform]" or "[Use Case] using [Service]" | ~55 characters | Automated Data Analytics on Cloud |

## Use Case Descriptions

Use case descriptions appear on landing pages and should be **75-80 words maximum**. They follow this structure:

1. **1-2 sentences** stating an existing challenge or opportunity for the customer
2. **1 sentence** introducing how the solutions address the challenge
3. **1-2 sentences** detailing business benefits (avoid getting too technical)

### Do NOT Include

- Customer names
- Negative or condescending language about the challenge
- Promises, assurances, or grand statements about what will be delivered
- Calls to action
- Jargon

## Writing Standards

### Voice and Tone

The voice is direct, informative, and approachable. We avoid convoluted language and state the point upfront. We provide useful content, not fluff. Friendly rather than formal, but professional enough to maintain trust.

### Conciseness

Use fewer words and the right ones. Test: imagine you pay for each word. Are there any unnecessary words you can cut?

**Do:** "Companies of all sizes want to predict future demand so they can improve the customer experience and increase revenue."

**Don't:** "Big, medium, and small companies want to not only understand what their customers are looking for but also what they really need, so they must accurately and precisely predict future demand to improve and enhance revenue and make the customer experience better."

### Weasel Words

Avoid vague words that introduce ambiguity: should, could, would, might, some, many, most, few, often, occasionally.

### Active Voice

Use active voice (subject-verb-object). Passive voice is wordier and often obscures details.

**Do:** "The streaming service sends data from social media feeds to the cloud."

**Don't:** "Data is captured from social media feeds and sent to the cloud."

### Inclusivity

| Don't use | Use instead |
|-----------|-------------|
| master | primary, main, leader |
| slave | replica, secondary, follower |
| whitelist | allowlist, approved list |
| blacklist | denylist, blocklist |
